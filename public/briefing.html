Executive narrative

RetailNext x OpenAI

Turning event intent into available outfits.

RetailNext customers are not just searching for products; they are shopping for moments. This briefing shows how OpenAI can interpret that intent while RetailNext keeps product, inventory, price, and fulfilment decisions grounded in its own systems.

01 What we heard

RetailNext is losing high-intent shoppers when style discovery and local availability are disconnected.

The discovery signal is specific: shoppers are leaving poor reviews because they cannot find updated styles or specific items in stores for upcoming events. That is a conversion problem, a store execution problem, and a customer trust problem.

Customer signal

"I know the event, not the SKU."

Customers describe intent: a wedding guest look, a first-day outfit, a last-minute party, or something current in store.

Friction point

Search and inventory answer separately.

A relevant recommendation still fails if the item is unavailable locally, not event-appropriate, or impossible to complete as a basket.

Business impact

Failed missions become poor reviews.

High-urgency shopping journeys create lower tolerance for friction, more associate lookup time, and less visibility into missed demand.

Lost conversionhigh-intent shoppers abandon when availability confidence is low
Manual lookupassociates bridge style, stock, substitutes, and customer messaging
Brand trustreview pain scales when store discovery does not match digital promise

02 Search gap

People shop with intention, but traditional search still behaves like a keyword matcher.

Event-based shopping carries context that keyword search misses: formality, weather, season, colour harmony, budget, urgency, and whether the shopper can actually find the look in a local store today.

Traditional search

Black dress

Returns product matches, then forces the shopper or associate to work out event fit, complementary items, substitutes, and availability.

Intent-led shopping

Smart outfit for a winter wedding in New York

Understands the mission, retrieves semantically relevant products, checks store stock, and explains a complete outfit.

Strategic shift

The opportunity is not another chatbot. It is an intent-to-inventory layer that turns natural language and images into governed, measurable retail actions.

03 Approach

RetailNext Event Stylist connects intent, catalogue retrieval, inventory grounding, and activation.

The prototype extends the OpenAI clothing RAG cookbook into an event-aware, inventory-aware stylist. OpenAI handles ambiguous judgement; RetailNext systems decide what is eligible, available, affordable, and safe to show.

UnderstandInterpret item image, event, style, budget, store, and urgency
RetrieveUse embeddings to find catalogue candidates by meaning, not keywords alone
GroundApply SKU truth: inventory, price, role, budget, fulfilment, and substitutes
ActivateRender basket, preview chat changes, brief associates, and trigger lifecycle follow-up
What OpenAI does

Converts ambiguity into usable retail intent.

Vision analysis, structured outputs, semantic search queries, chat-to-action reasoning, shopper explanations, and guardrail review.

What RetailNext controls

Owns the commercial truth.

Only catalogue SKUs are recommended. Price, stock, budget, fulfilment, substitutions, basket mutation, and CRM delivery stay deterministic.

06 Architecture

The model handles judgement; the application keeps authority over retail truth.

Click through the flow to show which components are OpenAI-powered and which remain deterministic RetailNext controls.

OpenAI API powered

  • Vision reads uploaded or selected starter-item images
  • Structured JSON turns styling ambiguity into item attributes, roles, and search intent
  • Embeddings create semantic vectors for event and product retrieval
  • Tool/action selection turns chat into explain, lookup, or preview operations
  • Generation writes shopper rationale, associate notes, email copy, and final review

RetailNext owned

  • Frontend form, upload, generation trigger, result rendering, and basket state
  • Catalogue, SKU metadata, product images, prices, and store inventory truth
  • Budget, local availability, urgency, role matching, substitution, and fulfilment validation
  • Preview/apply safety so Mira cannot mutate a basket until the app validates the change
  • Business KPIs, missed-demand signal, deployment, and CRM/Braze handoff
